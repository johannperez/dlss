# Rebuild Clase 4 from Spanish videos, your own notebook

No single online resource replaces Clase 4, but about two and a half hours of short, free, mostly Spanish material rebuilds its narration. The course's own notebook then supplies the parts nothing on the web covers. For the concepts ("dibujar vs calcar", train/val/test, reading loss curves, epochs and learning rate), Spanish coverage is good: DotCSV's overfitting pair, Codificando Bits' videos on data splits and loss curves, and the official Latin American Spanish (es-419) translation of Google's Machine Learning Crash Course (MLCC). TensorFlow Playground and MLU-Explain add visual, no-code interaction, though their interfaces are in English. The weak spot is PyTorch. No beginner Spanish resource walks through the `zero_grad` / `backward` / `step` loop, and the closest English one, Daniel Bourke's learnpytorch.io chapter 01, fits a straight line, which can't overfit. **No resource in either language combines a ReLU `nn.Sequential` network, a 1-D nonlinear regression, a train/val/test split and early stopping that saves the best weights.** So `clases/04-entrenar-de-verdad/notebook.ipynb` stays at the center of the catch-up, and the external resources work as stand-ins for the lost explanation around it. To recap Clases 1–4 as a whole, the best Spanish option is the official dub of 3Blue1Brown's chapters 1–2 (about 40 minutes) plus the MLCC es-419 pages. On the practical side, Godoy's free English Colab notebook follows the course's line → gradient descent → autograd → `nn.Linear` path most closely. One caveat applies throughout: durations, titles and caption tracks were checked against YouTube metadata, but **none of the videos was watched in full**, so the claims about their content rest on titles, descriptions, chapter lists and third-party summaries.

## Spanish videos cover "dibujar vs calcar" well; Spanish PyTorch does not exist at this level

DotCSV's "Las Redes Neuronales... ¿Aprenden o Memorizan? – Overfitting y Underfitting – Parte 1" (**10:18**, 2019) asks the same question Clase 4 asks: does the model learn the shape, or does it copy the points? That is the closest match in spirit to "dibujar vs calcar" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7-6X3DTt3R8)). Its follow-up, "Cómo identificar el OVERFITTING en tu RED NEURONAL – Parte 2" (**12:15**), promises "uno de los métodos para poder identificarlo" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmLKqZYlYUI)). Its title and the channel's arc suggest it covers validation sets, but nobody has confirmed that it shows train vs validation curves. DotCSV speaks peninsular Spanish, talks fast, and has only auto-generated captions. The videos are seven years old, but the concepts haven't changed.

Codificando Bits (Miguel Sotaquirá, Latin American Spanish) supplies the two pieces that map most directly onto the class's mechanics. "Los sets de entrenamiento, validación y prueba" (**10:33**, 2023) has chapters on parameters vs hyperparameters (00:55), the training set (03:06), the validation set (04:18) and the test set (06:40) ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=79K93XBOsIg)). "¿Por qué mi Red Neuronal NO APRENDE?: las curvas de pérdida" (**19:11**, April 2026) spends **05:32–17:41** on how to read loss curves, which is what the course's `curvas_de_entrenamiento` plots are for ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sEh39tFaoFQ)). The same channel's "LOTES y ÉPOCAS al entrenar una Red Neuronal" (**21:07**, 2025) covers batch and epoch vocabulary at greater length ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qLugt2MfKQw)). These videos are longer than DotCSV's, sometimes show Python code, and their descriptions advertise a paid course. For early stopping, the only Spanish source found is a University of Oviedo lecture clip, "Parada temprana (Early Stopping)" (**9:25**, 2023). It is pitched at data-science undergraduates, so it fits best as optional viewing ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kTK5eq6jmLc)).

The strongest Spanish text is Google's MLCC module "Conjuntos de datos, generalización y sobreajuste", officially translated to es-419 ([módulo](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/overfitting?hl=es-419)). Its data-splitting page puts Clase 4's "el test se toca una sola vez" in its own words: "Cuanto más uses el mismo conjunto de prueba, más probabilidades habrá de que el modelo se ajuste estrechamente al conjunto de prueba. Al igual que un profesor que 'enseña para la prueba'..." ([MLCC división](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/overfitting/dividing-datasets?hl=es-419)). The loss-curve page has figures of an ideal curve, oscillation, spikes, and the textbook overfitting pattern, where validation loss rises while training loss keeps falling ([MLCC curvas](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/overfitting/interpreting-loss-curves?hl=es-419)). The complexity page invokes Occam's razor and recommends starting from a simple baseline, "un modelo de referencia" ([MLCC complejidad](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/overfitting/model-complexity?hl=es-419)). A separate page, "Regresión lineal: Hiperparámetros", covers learning rate, batch size and epochs, with curves for learning rates that are too small and too large ([MLCC hiperparámetros](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/linear-regression/hyperparameters?hl=es-419)). The full module is estimated at **105 minutes** ([MLCC EN](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/overfitting)). Its examples lean toward classification and L2 regularization, which go beyond Clase 4, so students should read only selected pages.

The Spanish search turned up nothing usable for the PyTorch loop itself. Searches for Spanish PyTorch training-loop or regression tutorials returned no beginner material. The Spanish PyTorch options that do exist don't fit this audience. DotCSV Lab's "Introducción a PyTorch" is a **69-minute** 2020 livestream ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5YyDu5rO3kE)). Atecnea's 20-minute intro is unverified beyond its title ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8z_nCLy4WHc)). Platzi's PyTorch course is mostly paywalled, and its structure appears to be modeled on Bourke's chapter ([Platzi](https://platzi.com/cursos/pytorch/regresion-lineal-desde-cero-en-pytorch/)). Dive into Deep Learning (d2l) has no Spanish edition.

## Two English toys make overfitting visible without code

TensorFlow Playground is the most "drawing machine"-like resource found. In the browser, students control the train/test ratio, noise, batch size, learning rate and the number of neurons, choose a "Regression" problem, and watch "Training loss" and "Test loss" update live ([playground.tensorflow.org](https://playground.tensorflow.org/)). If they raise the noise, shrink the training share and add neurons, test loss climbs while training loss falls: "calcar" made visible. The interface is English-only, but it has few enough controls that one line of Spanish per control is enough to explain it. MLU-Explain's "Train, Test, and Validation Sets" is an equation-free interactive essay. Students drag training points to reshape a boundary and drag validation points to update the scores, and the essay explicitly warns that peeking at the test set early "is a form of overfitting" ([MLU-Explain](https://mlu-explain.github.io/train-test-validation/)). Companion essays on bias–variance and cross-validation also load ([bias-variance](https://mlu-explain.github.io/bias-variance/), [cross-validation](https://mlu-explain.github.io/cross-validation/)). There is no Spanish version.

StatQuest's "Bias and Variance" (**6:36**) fits the course's "formulas later or never" approach: according to a third-party summary, it compares a straight line with a "squiggly line" on a train/test split ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EuBBz3bI-aA); [summary](https://chaindesk.ai/tools/youtube-summarizer/machine-learning-fundamentals-bias-and-variance-EuBBz3bI-aA)). Its human caption tracks are English and Brazilian Portuguese only, so Spanish depends on YouTube's auto-translate (not confirmed to be enabled). The course's own `referencias.md` already recommends StatQuest and PyTorch's "Learn the Basics" but marks both `[VERIFICAR URL]`. This research confirmed those URLs ([StatQuest B&V](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EuBBz3bI-aA), [StatQuest CV](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fSytzGwwBVw), [PyTorch Optimizing Model Parameters](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/beginner/basics/optimization_tutorial.html)), so those entries can be updated.

## The PyTorch side has near misses, not a replacement

The closest external match for the loop is Bourke's learnpytorch.io "01. PyTorch Workflow Fundamentals". It builds a synthetic line (50 points, 80/20 train/test split) and runs a 100-epoch loop of forward → loss → `zero_grad()` → `backward()` → `step()` with `SGD(lr=0.01)`. It has a test loop and **plotted train/test loss curves**, and it opens in Colab ([learnpytorch.io/01](https://www.learnpytorch.io/01_pytorch_workflow/)). The mismatches matter for this class. It uses L1 loss rather than MSE, defines models as classes with hand-made `nn.Parameter`s, has no validation set, and, most importantly, **a two-parameter line can't overfit**, so it teaches the loop but not the "calcar" half. Its chapter 02 adds `nn.ReLU` inside `nn.Sequential` after showing that a linear model fails on nonlinear data, but on a classification problem (circles), not a regression ([learnpytorch.io/02](https://www.learnpytorch.io/02_pytorch_classification/)). The page is long, so students should be sent only to section 3 (the training loop and the curves).

PyTorch's official "Optimizing Model Parameters" explains the same three steps, including why `zero_grad()` is needed (gradients accumulate). It does so with FashionMNIST classification, a `DataLoader` and no validation set ([PyTorch docs](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/beginner/basics/optimization_tutorial.html)). Godoy's free Hugging Face notebook "A Beginner-Friendly PyTorch Tutorial" goes in the course's order: manual gradient descent in NumPy, then autograd, `nn.Linear` inside `Sequential`, `MSELoss` and SGD, and an 80/20 **train/validation** split. It doesn't plot the curves and has no ReLU ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/dvgodoy/beginner-pytorch-tutorial)). His full *PyTorch Step-by-Step* chapters 0–1 are free in English on Colab ([GitHub](https://github.com/dvgodoy/PyTorchStepByStep)); the Spanish edition is paid, at a minimum of US$9.95 ([Leanpub](https://leanpub.com/pytorch_ES)).

The gap is specific. **None of the resources combines a small ReLU regression network, MSE, Adam, a train/val/test split by index and early stopping with saved best weights on one dataset.** The core picks barely mention Adam. Bourke saves and loads a `state_dict`, but not as early stopping. The course notebook's approach (keep a `.clone()` "photo" of the best weights, documented in `clases/04-entrenar-de-verdad/pytorch-en-este-notebook.md`) has no external counterpart. Longer English options exist, but they are pitched at programmers or run long: Raschka's "PyTorch in One Hour" (classification, DataLoader, multi-GPU; [page](https://sebastianraschka.com/teaching/pytorch-1h/)), Karpathy's micrograd (**2 h 26 min**; [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMj-3S1tku0)) and d2l, whose wrapper classes hide raw PyTorch ([d2l.ai](https://d2l.ai/chapter_linear-regression/linear-regression-scratch.html)). None of them belongs on a catch-up list for this audience.

## One recap covers Clases 1–4 in Spanish: 3Blue1Brown plus MLCC

For the conceptual arc from the first class onward, 3Blue1Brown's "Deep learning" chapter 1 (**18:40**) and chapter 2 (**20:33**) have an official Spanish dubbed audio track and human Spanish subtitles ([cap. 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk), [cap. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IHZwWFHWa-w)). The separate "3Blue1Brown Español" channel hosts the same dubs ([cap. 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jKCQsndqEGQ), [cap. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwHiaTrQOiI)). Together they cover a network as a function with many knobs, cost as a single number, and gradient descent down a landscape. Their framing is MNIST digits with sigmoid neurons and some matrix notation, and they don't touch train/val/test. The best fit for the course's "add kinks to bend the drawing" metaphor is StatQuest's "The Essential Main Ideas of Neural Networks" (**18:54**). It has es-US subtitles and, per third-party summaries, fits data by adding bent lines into a "squiggle" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CqOfi41LfDw); [summary](https://www.classcentral.com/course/youtube-neural-networks-45644)).

MLCC es-419 is the only resource that spans all four classes in Spanish with interactive elements. Short Spanish alternatives per class: DotCSV's "La Neurona" (9:14; [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MRIv2IwFTPg)) and "La Red" (11:34; [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uwbHOpp9xkc)), DotCSV's "Descenso del Gradiente" (9:24; [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A6FiCDoz8_4)), and Iván Marqués' "valle de una cadena montañosa" (16:12), whose title matches the course's valley metaphor ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_USeAGPqqWs)). ## Lista para estudiantes: cómo recuperar la clase 4

> **Para quienes no pudieron venir a la clase 4.** La clase no quedó grabada, así que armamos este camino. Son unas **dos horas y media** en total. El corazón es el notebook de la clase; lo demás te cuenta con otras voces lo que se dijo en el aula. Hacelo en este orden.
>
> **1. ¿Aprende o memoriza? (10 min).** DotCSV, *¿Aprenden o Memorizan? – Overfitting y Underfitting, Parte 1*. Es la idea central de la clase: un modelo que **dibuja** la forma general contra uno que **calca** cada punto, ruido incluido. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7-6X3DTt3R8
>
> **2. Para qué partimos los datos en tres (11 min).** Codificando Bits, *Los sets de entrenamiento, validación y prueba*. Train para ajustar las perillas, validación para elegir, test una sola vez al final. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=79K93XBOsIg
>
> **3. Jugá a sobreajustar (15 min).** TensorFlow Playground, en el navegador y sin código. Elegí *Regression*, subí *Noise*, bajá *Ratio of training to test data*, agregá neuronas y apretá play. Mirá cómo *Training loss* baja mientras *Test loss* sube: eso es calcar. Después probá cambiar *Learning rate*. https://playground.tensorflow.org/
>
> **4. Leer las curvas de pérdida (12 min).** Codificando Bits, *¿Por qué mi Red Neuronal NO APRENDE?: las curvas de pérdida*. Mirá del minuto 5:32 al 17:41. Son las mismas curvas que dibuja el notebook. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sEh39tFaoFQ
>
> **5. Leer en castellano, con preguntitas (25 min).** Curso de Google, en español. Leé dos páginas: *División del conjunto de datos* (fijate lo del profesor que "enseña para la prueba") e *Interpretación de curvas de pérdida*. Lo que hable de clasificación o regularización, salteátelo.
> https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/overfitting/dividing-datasets?hl=es-419
> https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/overfitting/interpreting-loss-curves?hl=es-419
>
> **6. El notebook de la clase 4 (60–90 min). Esto no lo saltees.** Abrilo en Colab y corré celda por celda, con las slides al lado. Es lo único que junta todo lo de la clase: la red con ReLU, la partición train/val/test, el loop de PyTorch, las curvas y el *early stopping* que guarda los mejores pesos. Ningún recurso de afuera lo tiene completo. Si una línea te frena, buscala en el machete `pytorch-en-este-notebook.md`. Al final, hacé el cuestionario.

## What remains unverified, and what this means for the instructor

The verification is shallower than the list might suggest. Every URL above loaded, and YouTube durations come from the `lengthSeconds` field, so they are exact. But **no video was watched in full**. Content claims rest on titles, official descriptions, chapter markers and third-party summaries. The specific open questions: whether DotCSV Parte 2 actually shows train vs validation curves (the research notes disagree on this, so it is left out of the core path); the depth and code density of the Codificando Bits and Ringa Tech videos; whether YouTube auto-translates StatQuest into Spanish; the accent of 3Blue1Brown's Spanish dub (the track ID is plain "es"); and the exact timestamp of Bourke's chapter-01 walkthrough inside his 25-hour video ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z_ikDlimN6A?t=15419)). Nobody checked whether MLCC or MLU-Explain covers early stopping. Searches found no gentle resource on AIC/BIC or on "honest reporting of a small experiment". The searches were not exhaustive: there was no targeted GitHub search for Spanish university PyTorch notebooks, and fast.ai was not checked. Before sending the list, it's worth spot-checking items 1, 2 and 4 at 1.5× speed (about 25 minutes).

## Conclusion

The research shows a clean split. The *ideas* of Clase 4 are well served by free Spanish material, and the *mechanics* of the class (the PyTorch loop on a small nonlinear regression with honest validation and early stopping) exist only in the course's own notebook. That changes what a "recovery" assignment is: external videos rebuild the narration, but students who skip the notebook will have the vocabulary without the hands-on skill, and the skill is what Clase 5 builds on. Two cheap fixes follow. First, update `referencias.md`, since the StatQuest and PyTorch URLs it marks `[VERIFICAR URL]` are now confirmed. Second, the missing piece the web doesn't supply is a 10-minute narrated screen recording of the notebook's early-stopping cell. That would fill the one gap no external resource covers, and it would serve future cohorts as well.
