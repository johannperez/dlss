## 1. "If you watch only one thing": what are the best single recap options?

### Takeaway
The best single-sitting recap in Spanish is the official Spanish dub of 3Blue1Brown's "Deep learning" chapters 1 and 2 (about 40 min together). They cover the course arc almost exactly: a network as a function with many knobs, cost as a number, the cost landscape and gradient descent. The closest match to the "drawing machine with knobs" metaphor is StatQuest's "The Essential Main Ideas of Neural Networks" (19 min, English, with es-US subtitles). It shows a network bending and adding pieces of ReLU-style curves into a "squiggle" that fits data. For a 9-to-20-minute Spanish-only alternative, DotCSV's "¿Qué es una Red Neuronal?" parts 1 and 2 plus "¿Qué es el Descenso del Gradiente?" work well.

### Inferences
- **Top pick for the arc (classes 1–3):** the Spanish dub of 3B1B chapters 1 and 2. On the original 3B1B videos, students choose Spanish under Settings → Audio track, or they can use the 3Blue1Brown Español channel. Caveats: 3B1B frames everything as digit recognition (MNIST) with sigmoid neurons, and chapter 1 thinks in terms of "neurons and layers" rather than "functions with knobs". It also shows matrix notation, which a math-wary audience may find off-putting. It does not cover train/val/test or overfitting (class 4).
- **Best fit for the course metaphor (class 2):** StatQuest. A network that adds bent pieces together into a squiggle is practically "a drawing machine with knobs that adds kinks". Caveat: it is English only, with Spanish subtitles only (es-US). Josh Starmer's humor and singing put some people off and delight others.
- **Possible "one-hour pack" for recovering class 4 in Spanish:** 3B1B ch1 (dubbed, 19 min) + 3B1B ch2 (dubbed, 21 min) + DotCSV "¿Aprenden o memorizan?" (10 min, overfitting) + the MLU-Explain train/test/validation explorable. Together these cover all four classes.

## 2. Spanish-language resources (DotCSV, Codificando Bits, Ringa Tech, others)

### Takeaway
Plenty of Spanish material exists. DotCSV (Spain, 2018–2019) has the most coherent conceptual series for this arc: the neuron as linear regression, the network as stacked neurons with activation functions, gradient descent, overfitting and validation. Codificando Bits (Latin America) has recent, more detailed videos on how networks learn, batches and epochs, and loss curves (2025–2026). These fit class 4 especially well, though they are longer and lean toward programming. Ringa Tech (Latin America) has a clear intuitive overview and a detailed activation-function video.

## 3. English and interactive resources (Nielsen, Google MLCC, TensorFlow Playground, MLU-Explain, StatQuest)

### Takeaway
Google's Machine Learning Crash Course is fully available in Latin American Spanish (`?hl=es-419`). It covers linear regression, loss (including an explicit MSE vs MAE discussion with outliers), gradient descent, hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, epochs), overfitting, and neural networks ("linear operations on linear operations are still linear"), with interactive exercises. It is the strongest single text companion for all four classes. MLU-Explain (English) has the best slider/drag explorables for class 4. Nielsen ch. 4 is the canonical interactive "visual proof" of universality, but it is English only and fairly technical. TensorFlow Playground lets students literally turn the knobs of a network.

### Inferences
- **Per-class picks (best overall, Spanish-first):**
  - **Class 1 (machine with knobs, fitting a line, squared error):** StatQuest "Fitting a Line" (9 min, EN with subtitles) or DotCSV Parte 1 (9 min, ES). As text, MLCC "Regresión lineal" + "Pérdida" (ES). Explorable: MLU-Explain Linear Regression (EN).
  - **Class 2 (stacking linear collapses, ReLU kinks, universality):** StatQuest "Essential Main Ideas of NNs" (19 min) is the closest to the metaphor. Also MLCC "Funciones de activación" (ES, states the collapse). Nielsen ch. 4 for the visual universality proof (EN, interactive, but long and more mathematical; best treated as optional). TensorFlow Playground for turning knobs and seeing a linear network fail on the circle/spiral data until you add ReLU. Note that Nielsen builds his proof with sigmoid "steps"/bumps, not ReLU kinks; the idea is the same but the pieces differ.
  - **Class 3 (loss landscape, gradient descent, learning rate, MSE vs MAE):** 3B1B ch. 2 (dubbed ES, 21 min). DotCSV "Descenso del Gradiente" (9 min). MLCC "descenso de gradientes" + "Hiperparámetros" + the "Pérdida" page for MSE vs MAE (ES).
  - **Class 4 (training loop, epochs, batches, train/val/test, early stopping):** MLU-Explain Train/Test/Validation (drag explorable, EN). MLCC "Sobreajuste" and "Hiperparámetros" (ES). DotCSV overfitting parts 1 and 2 (ES). Codificando Bits "Lotes y épocas" and "curvas de pérdida" (ES, long). StatQuest "Bias and Variance" (7 min).
- **Explorables for a slider-driven style:** TensorFlow Playground (EN UI but very visual; the language barrier is low), MLCC's embedded interactive exercises (ES), MLU-Explain (EN) and Nielsen ch. 4 (EN).
- MLCC is the only resource that covers all four classes in Spanish with interactive elements. Its tone is aimed at future practitioners, but the text is mostly prose with minimal formulas.
